How this works

Finding renewal work

  1. Pick a time window — consents that expire inside it will need replacing, so their holders will need applications, assessments and reports.
  2. Narrow it down by council, type of work and type of holder. Small jobs (moorings, domestic bores, septic tanks) and farm effluent are hidden until you tick them.
  3. Open a consent to see its renewal deadline, the property it's on, council documents and other consents at the same site.
  4. Star the ones worth chasing. Your shortlist and notes stay in this browser; use Export to share them as a spreadsheet.

The Holders tab ranks consent holders by how many of their consents fall in your selection — a quick way to spot clients with several renewals coming up.

Renewal deadlines (RMA s124)

A holder who applies for a replacement consent at least 6 months before expiry can keep operating while the new application is processed. Between 6 and 3 months before expiry the council can still allow it; with less than 3 months to go there's no protection. Each consent shows its lodge by date.

Law changes that move expiry dates

  • 31 Dec 2027 RMA s123C (December 2025) extended most consents that were due to expire before 31 December 2027 to that date — which is why so many consents share it.
  • The Planning Act 2026 is expected to extend most consents again, to about two years after the national transition period ends (around 2031).
  • Firm marks expiry dates the extensions don't reach: freshwater consents (water permits and discharges that can reach water) at their 35-year maximum term, and wastewater network consents. These are the renewals most certain to happen on time.

This is a guide, not legal advice — confirm a consent's status with the council.

Property information

Zoom in and click anywhere on the map to see the land parcel there: legal description, area, record of title numbers and addresses, plus every consent on that parcel. Parcel boundaries appear when you zoom in close.

Parcel, title and address data is from Toitū Te Whenua LINZ (CC BY 4.0). Owner names aren't shown: LINZ only releases them under its Licence for Personal Data, which doesn't allow unsolicited direct marketing. Order a title from LINZ or use your licensed property tools when you need the owner.

Know the gaps

  • Only Bay of Plenty and Waikato publish holder names. Elsewhere the holder type is guessed from what the consent is for (marked likely).
  • Northland publishes no expiry dates, so its consents only appear under All. Taranaki publishes current consents only.
  • Types of work are worked out from each council's own wording, so a few consents will be in the wrong group.
  • Data is downloaded from each council's public map service every day; the Data button shows when each council last updated.
